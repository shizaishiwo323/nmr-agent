## Conclusions usable by the agent

### Diffusive coupling and inter-pore exchange
- **When to use**: When several expected pore populations merge into one broad peak in the inversion, the peak position falls between geometric predictions, the connected-pore model is smoother than the isolated-pore model, or 1D T2 cannot separate pore populations.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: Spins that diffuse between pores of different sizes average over several local relaxation environments; when coupling is strong, the T2 distribution no longer equals the independent responses of the individual pore-size groups.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: Peak merging or spectral broadening does not necessarily mean that only one pore population exists; it can also result from inter-pore diffusive coupling that mixes several relaxation environments. [@Song2014PoreCouplingReview]

### T2-T2/T1-T2 multidimensional NMR and exchange
- **When to use**: When a 1D T2 spectrum cannot distinguish coupling/exchange, fluid components or complex pore environments, and it is necessary to explain that 2D NMR provides stronger constraints.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: Two-dimensional correlation experiments encode relaxation correlations across different waiting/mixing periods into a 2D spectrum, which can reveal exchange, coupling and links between different fluid or pore environments.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: In complex samples, 1D T2 peaks cannot always be uniquely assigned to physical pore populations, and multidimensional NMR can serve as a check and an additional constraint. [@Song2014PoreCouplingReview]

### Surface relaxation, pore size and short T2
- **When to use**: When the short-T2 area is large, the main peak shifts toward short T2, small-pore or high-surface-area models decay faster, or the relation between T2 and pore size needs explaining.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: Effective transverse relaxation is usually controlled jointly by bulk relaxation, surface relaxation and diffusion-related terms; under the fast-diffusion approximation, small pores or pores with a high surface-area-to-volume ratio show shorter T2.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: A short-T2 component generally supports an interpretation of stronger surface relaxation, smaller pores or a higher surface-area-to-volume ratio, but this must be judged together with the surface relaxivity and the diffusion regime. [@Song2014PoreCouplingReview]
