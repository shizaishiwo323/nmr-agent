# KeatingKnight2007IronOxides

- **Title**: A laboratory study to determine the effect of iron oxides on proton NMR measurements
- **Suggested citation format**: `[@KeatingKnight2007IronOxides]`
- **AGU reference**: Keating, K., & Knight, R. (2007). A laboratory study to determine the effect of iron oxides on proton NMR measurements. Geophysics, 72(1), E27–E32. https://doi.org/10.1190/1.2399445
- **Reason for inclusion**: Coats quartz sand with five iron oxides and quantitatively compares surface relaxivity and internal-gradient effects, directly relevant to unconsolidated geological materials.
- **Role in the T2 agent**: Reference for geomaterials: effect of the iron-oxide mineral type on surface relaxivity and T2.

## Conclusions usable by the agent

### Iron oxides shorten T2
- **When to invoke**: When the sample is an iron-bearing sand or soil, a weathered soil or a red-bed material, when T2 is short overall, or when the user wants to convert T2 directly to pore size.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: Iron-oxide coatings raise the relaxation rate of water-saturated quartz sand, mainly by increasing the surface relaxation rate; differences between minerals arise from both S/V and the surface relaxivity ρ2. In Table 2, ρ2 rises from about 0.31 μm/s for quartz sand to about 17.9 μm/s for hematite and about 292 μm/s for magnetite; magnetite additionally accelerates relaxation through internal gradients.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: Iron-bearing minerals markedly raise the surface relaxivity (about 0.31 μm/s for quartz sand, about 17.9 μm/s for hematite coatings), so a short T2 in iron-bearing geomaterials cannot be interpreted directly as smaller pores [@KeatingKnight2007IronOxides].

## Limits of use

- The samples are artificially coated quartz sands with an iron content of about 1% (0.65% for magnetite); natural soils have more complex mineralogy, so the values serve only as an order-of-magnitude reference.
- Crossref records a publication year of 2006 (online), while the journal volume/issue is volume 72, issue 1 of 2007; cite using the journal volume/issue.
