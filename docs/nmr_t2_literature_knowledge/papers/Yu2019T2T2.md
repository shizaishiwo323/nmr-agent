# Yu2019T2T2

- **Title**: Characterization of porous media by T2-T2 correlation beyond fast diffusion limit
- **Suggested citation format**: `[@Yu2019T2T2]`
- **Rationale for inclusion**: Interpretation of T2/T2 and related 2D NMR; supports the analysis of complex spectral shapes and peak groups.
- **Role in the T2 agent**: Citation for pore coupling and multidimensional NMR interpretation; useful for explaining peak merging, exchange and the non-uniqueness of 1D T2 interpretation.

## Conclusions usable by the agent

### T2-T2/T1-T2 multidimensional NMR and exchange
- **When to use**: When a 1D T2 spectrum cannot distinguish coupling/exchange, fluid components or complex pore environments, and it is necessary to explain that 2D NMR provides stronger constraints.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: Two-dimensional correlation experiments encode relaxation correlations across different waiting/mixing periods into a 2D spectrum, which can reveal exchange, coupling and links between different fluid or pore environments.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: In complex samples, 1D T2 peaks cannot always be uniquely assigned to physical pore populations, and multidimensional NMR can serve as a check and an additional constraint. [@Yu2019T2T2]

### Ill-posedness of the inversion and regularization
- **When to use**: When the inverted T2 spectrum shows noise oscillations or spurious peaks, when changing the regularization factor clearly changes the number or width of peaks, or when the need for NNLS/smoothness constraints must be explained.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: Recovering a T2 distribution from a CPMG decay is a multi-exponential, inverse-Laplace-type problem in which noise and the sampling range are amplified; non-negativity and smoothness regularization trade off the fit residual against spectral smoothness.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: The T2 spectrum is not a unique direct reading of the decay curve but an inversion result shaped by noise, the sampling window and regularization constraints. [@Yu2019T2T2]

### Surface relaxation, pore size and short T2
- **When to use**: When the short-T2 area is large, the main peak shifts toward short T2, small-pore or high-surface-area models decay faster, or the relation between T2 and pore size needs explaining.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: Effective transverse relaxation is usually controlled jointly by bulk relaxation, surface relaxation and diffusion-related terms; under the fast-diffusion approximation, small pores or pores with a high surface-area-to-volume ratio show shorter T2.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: A short-T2 component generally supports an interpretation of stronger surface relaxation, smaller pores or a higher surface-area-to-volume ratio, but this must be judged together with the surface relaxivity and the diffusion regime. [@Yu2019T2T2]
