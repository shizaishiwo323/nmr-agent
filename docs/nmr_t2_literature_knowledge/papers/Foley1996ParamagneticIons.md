# Foley1996ParamagneticIons

- **Title**: Effect of Paramagnetic Ions on NMR Relaxation of Fluids at Solid Surfaces
- **Suggested citation format**: `[@Foley1996ParamagneticIons]`
- **AGU reference**: Foley, I., Farooqui, S. A., & Kleinberg, R. L. (1996). Effect of paramagnetic ions on NMR relaxation of fluids at solid surfaces. Journal of Magnetic Resonance, Series A, 123, 95–104. https://doi.org/10.1006/jmra.1996.0218
- **Reason for inclusion**: Uses synthetic calcium silicate powders with known iron/manganese concentrations to show that surface relaxivity increases linearly with paramagnetic ion concentration and that oxide surfaces remain appreciably relaxing without paramagnetic ions.
- **Role in the T2 agent**: Mechanism reference: quantitative relation between surface relaxivity and paramagnetic ion concentration.

## Conclusions usable by the agent

### Surface relaxivity as a function of paramagnetic ion concentration
- **When to invoke**: When samples of similar geometry show clearly different T2 peak positions, or when it needs to be stated that the surface relaxivity ρ is not constant.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: In water-saturated powders the proton relaxation rate is linear in the concentration of iron/manganese paramagnetic ions; even without paramagnetic ions, oxide surfaces are themselves effective relaxation sources. The measured T1/T2 agree with values observed in rock studies.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: Surface relaxivity increases linearly with the paramagnetic ion content of the pore wall, so T2 differences between samples may arise from mineral chemistry rather than pore size [@Foley1996ParamagneticIons].

## Limits of use

- The samples are synthetic calcium silicate powders serving as a model of oxide surfaces in sandstone; extrapolation to clay-rich or organic-rich soils requires caution.
- When the same phenomenon can be caused by several mechanisms, prefer wording such as "may", "suggests" or "supports" over absolute statements.
