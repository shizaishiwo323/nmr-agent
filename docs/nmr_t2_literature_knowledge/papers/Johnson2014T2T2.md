## Conclusions usable by the agent

### T2-T2/T1-T2 multidimensional NMR and exchange
- **When to invoke**: When a 1D T2 spectrum cannot distinguish coupling/exchange, fluid components or complex pore environments, and it must be stated that 2D NMR can provide stronger constraints.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: Two-dimensional correlation experiments encode relaxation correlations across different waiting/mixing periods into a 2D spectrum, which can reveal exchange, coupling and links between different fluid/pore environments.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: In complex samples, 1D T2 peaks cannot always be assigned uniquely to physical pore populations; multidimensional NMR can serve as validation and an additional constraint. [@Johnson2014T2T2]

### Diffusive coupling and inter-pore exchange
- **When to invoke**: When several expected pore populations merge into one broad peak in the inversion, when peak positions fall between the geometric predictions, when a connected-pore model gives a smoother spectrum than an isolated-pore model, or when 1D T2 cannot separate pore populations.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: Spins that diffuse between pores of different sizes average several local relaxation environments; when coupling is strong, the T2 distribution no longer equals the independent response of each pore-size population.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: Peak merging or spectral broadening does not necessarily mean that only one pore population is present; it may result from inter-pore diffusive coupling that mixes several relaxation environments. [@Johnson2014T2T2]
