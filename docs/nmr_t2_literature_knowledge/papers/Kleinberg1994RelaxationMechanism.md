# Kleinberg1994RelaxationMechanism

- **Title**: Mechanism of NMR Relaxation of Fluids in Rock
- **Suggested citation format**: `[@Kleinberg1994RelaxationMechanism]`
- **AGU reference**: Kleinberg, R. L., Kenyon, W. E., & Mitra, P. P. (1994). Mechanism of NMR relaxation of fluids in rock. Journal of Magnetic Resonance, Series A, 108, 206–214. https://doi.org/10.1006/jmra.1994.1112
- **Reason for inclusion**: Summarizes the mechanisms of relaxation of pore fluids in rock: dominance of surface paramagnetic ions, correspondence between the relaxation spectrum and the S/V spectrum in the fast-diffusion regime, and typical values of T1/T2 and surface relaxivity.
- **Role in the T2 agent**: Mechanism reference: conditions under which T2 maps to S/V, paramagnetic ions, typical T1/T2 range, and mineral heterogeneity.

## Conclusions usable by the agent

### Conditions under which the T2 spectrum maps to pore S/V
- **When to invoke**: When a report needs to link T2 peak positions to "large/small pores", or when the user asks whether T2 can be converted to pore size.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: In the fast-diffusion (surface-limited) regime, the decay within a single pore is single-exponential and depends only on S/V, so the relaxation spectrum corresponds to the S/V spectrum; in the slow-diffusion regime (large pores or strong surface relaxation) a single pore is also multi-exponential and depends on pore shape. In centrifuge experiments the longest T1 component disappears first, supporting the view that long relaxation corresponds to large pores.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: In the fast-diffusion regime, the T2 distribution can be mapped approximately onto the distribution of pore surface-to-volume ratio, and long-T2 components generally correspond to larger pores, but this correspondence requires the fast-diffusion assumption to hold [@Kleinberg1994RelaxationMechanism].

### Effect of minerals and paramagnetic ions on T2
- **When to invoke**: When the sample contains iron/manganese minerals or clay, or when T2 is clearly shorter than expected for the same pore size.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: The most important surface relaxation mechanism is the hyperfine interaction with paramagnetic ions such as Mn2+ and Fe3+ on grain surfaces; mineral composition can vary from pore to pore, in which case the heterogeneity reflected in the relaxation distribution is not necessarily related to pore size. The ρ1 of pure silica glass is several orders of magnitude lower than that of clean sandstone.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: The T2 distribution is controlled jointly by pore size and pore-wall minerals (especially paramagnetic ions); when the mineralogy is heterogeneous, T2 differences do not necessarily reflect differences in pore size [@Kleinberg1994RelaxationMechanism].
- **Evidence location**: See the evidence anchors below. Check against the original publication before citing.

### Typical T1/T2 values
- **When to invoke**: When the user provides T1–T2 results or asks whether a T1/T2 ratio is anomalous.
- **Physical/mathematical mechanism**: At 2 MHz, for 48 rock samples, T1/T2 lies between 1 and 2.6 with a median of 1.59; the ρ1 of clean sandstone is about 3×10⁻³ cm/s.
- **Citable sentence for reports**: At low field, T1/T2 of pore water in rock typically lies between 1 and 2.6 (median about 1.59 for 48 rock samples); a marked departure from this range should prompt consideration of internal gradients or special surface effects [@Kleinberg1994RelaxationMechanism].

## Evidence anchors

- p.1 (abstract): Paramagnetic ions are the main mechanism; relaxation rates depend only weakly on temperature; T1/T2 is clearly greater than 1 at low frequency.
- p.2: For 48 rock samples T1/T2 = 1–2.6 with a median of 1.59 (2 MHz); ρ1 of clean sandstone ≈ 3×10⁻³ cm/s; the distribution is almost independent of temperature between 25 and 175 °C.
- p.3: Definitions of the fast- and slow-diffusion regimes; when the mineralogy varies from pore to pore, the relaxation distribution reflects heterogeneity unrelated to pore size.

## Limits of use

- The authors describe the theory as semi-quantitative and the samples are sedimentary rocks; for soils or loose geomaterials use it only as a mechanistic reference.
- The numerical ranges (T1/T2, ρ1) are statistics of that set of rock samples and must not be used as universal thresholds.
- When the same phenomenon can be caused by several mechanisms, prefer wording such as "may", "suggests" or "supports" over absolute statements.
